Build-loop research packet · 2026-08-15

Seven-Repo Data Intelligence Audit

Seven repositories were graded on the same seven-part rubric — collecting data, scoring it, linking it, finding insights, writing outputs at different lengths, choosing which model runs, and working inside Claude Code and Codex — to see how they could combine into one research workflow.

Recommended flow

The research plugin already answers basic questions well and should keep doing exactly that. What you do not have is a data-analysis pipeline for the complex, multi-stage jobs — tracking a company's quarterly earnings, sizing a new market or business opportunity, reading the financial disclosures behind a merger, or working through a personal decision like which town to move to or which school to pick. You already own a module for nearly every stage of that pipeline, built two to five times over in two languages; the one thing nobody has is a way to link people, companies and products across documents, where the best score anywhere is 2 out of 3.

Spine: A thin coordinator runs focused stage agents; each stage reuses the strongest existing module and the research plugin remains the store for facts and sources.

  1. Collect data

    Hybrid

    A collector agent gathers the filings, pages and files a question needs and writes what it finds into the research plugin's store. A background job runs saved searches and feeds on a schedule, so long-running topics keep updating themselves.

    From: The collector agent reuses stratagem's SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission) and web collectors plus Omniparse file parsing, and borrows stratagem's extractor-specialist shape; the scheduled feed reader is new.

    Why: Every reader already exists and is tested, and stratagem is the only repo that reaches company filings. Only the schedule is missing — nothing here runs unattended today.

  2. Assess and score

    Existing

    An assessor agent rates how trustworthy each source is, checks the claims behind it, and marks the entry with one confidence level. It grades finished outputs against the same rubric before they leave the pipeline.

    From: The assessor agent reuses the research plugin's scoring and claim verification unchanged, plus the market research platform's five-point quality rubric, and borrows stratagem's source-verifier specialist shape.

    Why: The research plugin is the only mature scorer here, it runs without a model, and it already writes its verdict back onto the entry.

  3. Link and structure

    New

    A linker agent records the people, companies and products each entry mentions and how they relate, matches the same thing across entries, and exports the result as data plus a simple web view.

    From: The linker agent is new: entity and relationship tables on top of the research plugin's existing store, with the market research platform's vector store bolted on for search by meaning.

    Why: Nothing existing does this. The market research platform defines a graph but never runs it, and the research plugin links notes to each other rather than the things inside them.

  4. Find insights

    Hybrid

    An analyst agent compares stored facts across entries: the same measure with different values becomes a contradiction to check, a thing with sources but no claims becomes a gap, and a measure with dates becomes a trend. Anything unsupported goes back for another research pass.

    From: The analyst agent reuses local-smartz's fact-check-and-redo loop and stratagem's contradiction and trend prompts, and borrows local-smartz's analyser-then-fact-checker shape; the deterministic comparison over verified facts is new.

    Why: Both donors work, but they only narrate. A deterministic pass finds the candidates first, so the model explains rather than discovers.

  5. Write outputs (long, short, highlights, slides)

    Existing

    A writer agent takes the length you asked for — long report, short report, highlights, or a deck as an outline with slide text — picks the matching template and renders the file. A critic pass grades the draft before it comes back to you.

    From: The writer agent reuses stratagem's Markdown, PowerPoint (PPTX), Word (DOCX) and web-page renderers plus the market research platform's email, notes and outline writers, and borrows stratagem's synthesiser-then-critic shape.

    Why: Between them, stratagem and the market research platform already cover every format anyone here produces, including the only real PowerPoint (PPTX) file.

  6. Run models (local or cloud)

    Existing

    Unattended runs pin a model to each stage agent, prefer your own machine, and refuse cloud calls when the settings say local only. Inside Claude Code or Codex there is no runner at all — the assistant makes the calls.

    From: The model-runner reuses local-smartz's provider router with the market research platform's Apple-silicon runner (MLX) added, and keeps local-smartz's per-role model pinning.

    Why: local-smartz's router is the most complete here and fails safe, and Apple-silicon support costs one more runner rather than a rewrite.

  7. Work inside Claude Code / Codex

    Existing

    A thin coordinator decides which stage agents a question needs and runs them in order, each with its own skill and specialty. Simple questions skip the pipeline and stay with the research plugin's existing commands.

    From: The coordinator ships inside the research plugin's existing Claude Code and Codex packaging — its commands, one skill per stage agent, and the save hook.

    Why: The packaging already works in both hosts, so the stage agents ship as skills beside commands people already use, with nothing new to install.

Reference pipelines to borrow from

  • Perplexity Deep Research

    It runs a loop: search, read the documents it found, reason about what is still missing, refine the plan, and repeat across dozens of searches and hundreds of sources before writing a cited report. Most runs finish in two to four minutes.

    Borrow: Refine the plan as you learn instead of executing a fixed list of sub-questions.

    Source

  • AlphaSense Deep Research and Smart Summaries

    It searches a licensed financial corpus (filings, earnings-call transcripts, broker research, expert-call transcripts, and your uploaded documents), reasons over thousands of hits, and writes a report where every line links to the exact snippet in the source document.

    Borrow: Snippet-level citations that jump to the exact sentence in the source, and a source-priority order that puts filings and transcripts first.

    Source

  • OpenAI Deep Research

    It clarifies the ask, then plans and issues its own web searches, reads pages and PDFs, shows its reasoning as it decides which claims to verify next, can run code for data work, and returns a structured report with machine-readable citation spans.

    Borrow: Citations as structured objects (character span, title, URL) so downstream renderers and verifiers can check them, plus a code step for any calculation.

    Source

  • Google Gemini Deep Research

    It drafts a multi-point research plan the user can edit and approve, then browses the web (and optionally the user's mail and drive), shows its reasoning as it iterates, and delivers a multi-page report that exports to Docs.

    Borrow: A user-editable plan checkpoint before any search budget is spent.

    Source

Next actions

  1. Confirm the shape: a thin coordinator that runs one focused agent per stage, with the research plugin kept for basic questions and as the store for facts and sources.
  2. Build the one missing stage first — the linker that records people, companies and products and how they connect — because no existing pipeline does it and every complex job (earnings, market entry, mergers) needs it.
  3. Stand up the collector, assessor, writer and model-runner agents from the named existing modules, each as a skill beside the research plugin's commands, and add the scheduled feed reader so long-running topics update themselves.
  4. Borrow from the reference pipelines: refine the plan as you learn (Perplexity), let the user approve the plan before searching (Gemini), cite the exact snippet not just the page (AlphaSense), and keep citations machine-checkable (OpenAI).
  5. Retire market-intel after saving its two useful pieces, and work the open findings already filed in Operations Center.

market-research App

Web app + `mra` command line + Mac shell

You hand it files or web addresses, it extracts the text, splits it into chunks, and indexes them locally for keyword and meaning-based search. A two-pass summariser then clusters the best chunks by theme, merges them, and returns a conclusion-first summary with citations, either in the browser or from the command line.

●●○ 2 working but narrow

The read, index and summarise path genuinely works offline end to end, but it produces one shape of answer and the browser interface cannot export it.

Show flow
  1. collect

    It reads PDF, Excel, CSV, PowerPoint (PPTX) and Word (DOCX) files and scrapes pages, sending sensitive documents to a local parser instead of a cloud one.

    ●●● Broad format coverage and a real privacy rule, but only when you ask — nothing is scheduled.

  2. assess

    It scores each parse for confidence, tags themes, and drops near-duplicates within a run.

    ●●○ Useful signals about the parse, but no judgement about the source.

  3. structure

    It groups chunks by theme and stores them in a local vector database, with citations pointing back to chunk numbers.

    ●○○ Grouping is not linking — nothing connects the same company or product across documents.

  4. insight

    It asks the model, in one line of prompt, to point out where excerpts disagree.

    ●○○ An instruction, not a detector.

  5. synthesize

    It writes a conclusion with three to five supporting arguments and cited evidence, exportable as Word (DOCX) or JSON from the command line.

    ●●○ Strong single shape, but no short, highlight or slide variants, and no export from the browser.

  6. models

    It routes between Ollama on your machine, OpenAI and Groq after a health check, and can be forced local-only.

    ●●● Real choice with per-model prices and fallbacks, plus a reranker running in the same process.

  7. agents

    An agent runs its `mra` command line or calls the local web routes.

    ●○○ Clean entry points, but nothing registers with Claude Code or Codex.

market-research-platform Platform

Web platform: Python API + React front end

Documents are uploaded or scraped, read in Python, chunked, embedded, and stored in a single local database that serves both keyword and meaning-based search. A map-and-reduce pass turns the retrieved chunks into one structured brief, and five writers turn that brief into finished documents.

●●○ 2 working but narrow

The path from upload to finished document works and produces the widest range of outputs here, but the knowledge-graph half never runs and its test suite was not executed for this audit.

Show flow
  1. collect

    It takes uploads and reads PDF, Word (DOCX), Excel, PowerPoint (PPTX) and scanned images, and fetches pages through a small Node helper.

    ●●● The widest reading list here, scans included, but it only runs when asked.

  2. assess

    It rates source domains into four trust tiers, checks whether each claim is supported, and grades the finished brief on five dimensions.

    ●●○ Deterministic and reusable, and it needs no model to run.

  3. structure

    It embeds chunks into a local vector store, but the entity graph it defines never runs.

    ○○○ No live code path reaches the graph, so the store stays empty and no relationship is ever written.

  4. insight

    It pulls themes, claims, numbers, risks and actions out of the text with the surrounding context re-attached.

    ●○○ Extraction only — the comparison and trend prompts sit in the dead graph code.

  5. synthesize

    One brief feeds five writers: PowerPoint (PPTX), Word (DOCX), email, meeting notes and outline.

    ●●● The broadest set of finished documents here, all driven from a single structure.

  6. models

    One interface covers Ollama, Apple-silicon local models (MLX) and Claude, with cloud calls blocked unless you allow them.

    ●●○ Solid design, but the Apple-silicon path is only half connected and can be sent the wrong model name.

  7. agents

    An agent calls its web service after reading a handoff document written in prose.

    ●○○ No plugin, no commands and no tool server.

Omniparse Library

Published TypeScript SDK and command-line tool

You give it a file or a folder, it opens the file format directly, and it returns the same result shape every time: markdown, plain text, structure and a token estimate. Nothing is stored, scored or summarised — it hands the text to whatever called it.

●●○ 2 working but narrow

It does one job reliably and is already bundled inside the research plugin, but it stops at extraction, so on its own it answers no research question.

Show flow
  1. collect

    It reads local Excel, PowerPoint (PPTX), Python and PDF files, and whole folders, several at a time.

    ●●○ Fast and dependable for those formats, but it rejects web addresses and cannot read scans.

  2. assess

    It labels a file by its extension and estimates its token cost.

    ●○○ That is classification, not judgement — nothing is scored, tagged or de-duplicated.

  3. structure

    It captures deep structure inside one document, down to merged cells, charts, comments and slide notes.

    ●○○ Nothing connects one document to another.

  4. insight

    It does not do this.

    ○○○ The nearest thing is a count of what a spreadsheet contains, which describes the file rather than saying anything about it.

  5. synthesize

    Each reader can emit markdown, plain text or JSON, plus sized chunks meant for search.

    ●○○ Format conversion, not writing — no template decides how long the output should be.

  6. models

    It does not do this.

    ○○○ There is no model, embedding or provider code anywhere in it.

  7. agents

    An agent runs the command line or imports the published package.

    ●○○ Easy to call, but it registers with neither Claude Code nor Codex.

local-smartz App

Python app with command line, local server, and Mac app

A question goes to a planner, which hands it to a researcher, an analyser, a fact-checker and a writer in turn, each on a model you pin per role. The researcher searches and scrapes, the analyser computes every number by running code, the fact-checker sends the draft back if facts are missing, and the writer saves a Markdown, web page or Word (DOCX) report.

●●○ 2 working but narrow

The agent loop and the model router are both mature and heavily tested, but nothing is remembered between runs in a searchable form, so every question starts from zero.

Show flow
  1. collect

    Its agents scrape pages, search the web, and read PDF, spreadsheet and text files on request.

    ●●○ Good coverage for a live question, but there is no schedule, watcher or database connector.

  2. assess

    It grades its own drafts with a pass-or-needs-more verdict and a four-axis rubric.

    ●○○ It judges output quality and never the trustworthiness of what it read.

  3. structure

    It does not do this.

    ○○○ Results are flat JSON files, and the embedding models in its catalogue are never used.

  4. insight

    The fact-checker names missing facts and the pipeline re-runs the researcher up to twice, while the analyser compares sources and flags outliers.

    ●●○ The loop is wired into the code, not just requested in a prompt.

  5. synthesize

    It writes Markdown, web page and Word (DOCX) reports and spreadsheets from one template.

    ●●○ Real files, but PowerPoint is a text outline only and there is no email or memo form.

  6. models

    One interface covers Ollama on your machine plus Anthropic, OpenAI and Groq, with a model pinned per role and a privacy switch that fails closed.

    ●●● The most complete model layer here, with retries, cost estimates and picks that match your machine's memory.

  7. agents

    It hosts Claude Code plugins and speaks the tool-server protocol (MCP) as a client, and is itself called as a command or over its web interface.

    ●●○ Both of those are switched off on the default path, so most runs never see them.

research-plugin Plugin

Claude Code + Codex plugin, installed in both

The assistant does the reading and the writing; the plugin does everything deterministic around it. Findings become three-layer markdown notes, indexed in a small local database, scored for source trust and claim support, linked to related notes, and re-checked for staleness as they age.

●●● 3 mature or central

It is the only tool here in daily use with a live library, and every step it owns runs without a model, so it works the same in either host.

Show flow
  1. collect

    It reads PDF, Excel, PowerPoint (PPTX), CSV and Python files through a bundled copy of Omniparse, and links whole project folders.

    ●●○ Solid local intake, but it never searches the web itself and the only automatic trigger re-reads a file after you save it.

  2. assess

    It tiers source domains, blocks known misinformation sites, picks a research depth with a required mix of sources, and verifies claims four ways.

    ●●● The most complete scoring here, and it writes the verdict back onto the entry as one confidence mark.

  3. structure

    It turns wiki-style links into a two-way backlink list and rebuilds topic, tag and project hub pages.

    ●●○ Real linking between notes, but it never names the people or companies inside them and cannot search by meaning.

  4. insight

    Contradiction, gap and trend hunting are written instructions the assistant is told to follow.

    ●○○ The only automatic signals are how stale an entry is and how certain its input data looked.

  5. synthesize

    It supplies five report templates, three writing modes, and checkable rules for what each output must contain.

    ●●○ Strong guidance, but nothing renders a slide deck or a document file — every output is markdown the assistant writes.

  6. models

    It does not do this, and that is deliberate.

    ○○○ The written rule is that the assistant hosting the plugin does all model work, which keeps the plugin dependency-free.

  7. agents

    It installs as a plugin in both Claude Code and Codex with 22 commands, a skill, and a save hook.

    ●●● The only tool here that is first-class in both hosts, with one code path serving each.

stratagem App

Python app; its Claude Code plugin packaging was removed

A question is broken into parts and handed to twelve specialist agents that read documents, scrape pages and pull company filings, each writing findings to files the next agent picks up. A synthesiser and a critic turn those findings into structured sections, which one writer set renders as Markdown, PowerPoint (PPTX), Word (DOCX) or a web page. What it learns is saved as observations and fed back into later runs.

●●○ 2 working but narrow

The research method and the document writers are the strongest here, but it only talks to one model provider and its plugin packaging was deleted, so nothing installs into a host.

Show flow
  1. collect

    Thirteen tools scrape pages, read documents, and search and download SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission) filings, with read-only results cached.

    ●●● The only repo that reaches company filings, though nothing runs on a schedule.

  2. assess

    It records observations with a category, a confidence and tags, drops exact duplicates, and promotes agents that stay confident across runs.

    ●●○ It grades its own work well; the trustworthiness of a source is graded by prompt, not by code.

  3. structure

    It keeps flat topic indexes, a file manifest and a log of observations.

    ●○○ The only graph it draws is a map of its own code, not of what it read.

  4. insight

    Prompts grade each claim as verified, supported, unsourced, contradicted or stale, compare sources, track multiple periods, and watch for quality drifting down.

    ●●○ Genuine capability, but all of it depends on the model following instructions.

  5. synthesize

    One set of structured sections renders to Markdown, PowerPoint (PPTX), Word (DOCX) or a web page, with a slide builder that handles titles, bullets and tables.

    ●●● The only repo that writes a real deck file rather than a text outline.

  6. models

    You can pick a larger or smaller Claude model per agent, and change it while a run is going.

    ●○○ Provider choice is fixed to Anthropic by the software kit it is built on.

  7. agents

    An agent runs its command line or its web interface; the tool server still works if you wire it by hand.

    ●○○ The plugin manifests, commands and skills were deleted, and the documentation still points at them.

market-intel Prototype

It shows what a news-brief product would look like: a source list, a grid of articles, and a summary panel. None of it is real — the articles, the categories and the summaries are all fixed text in the source, and every button waits a second or two to imitate work before showing the same values back. No network request is ever made.

market-research App

You give it files or web addresses and it reads them, splits the text into chunks, and indexes them on your machine so it can find the right passages by keyword and by meaning. It then clusters the best passages by theme, merges them in two passes, and returns a conclusion with three to five supporting arguments and cited evidence. Anything that looks confidential is kept away from the cloud parser.

market-research-platform Platform

You upload documents or paste web addresses, and it reads them — including scanned pages — chunks the text, and indexes it in one local database that supports both keyword and meaning-based search. It then builds a single structured brief from the retrieved chunks and rates the sources and claims behind it. Five separate writers turn that one brief into finished documents.

Omniparse Library

You point it at a file or a folder and it opens the file format directly and returns the text, the structure, and an estimate of how many tokens the result will cost. Inside a spreadsheet it captures merged cells, links, named ranges, charts, comments and embedded images; inside a deck it captures slide text and speaker notes. It never scores, links, or summarises anything.

local-smartz App

You ask it a research question and five specialist roles work the answer in turn: plan, research, analyse, fact-check, write. It searches and scrapes the web, reads PDF, spreadsheet and text files, and computes every number by running code rather than asserting it. If the fact-checker says facts are missing, the work goes back to the researcher, up to twice.

research-plugin Plugin

It saves what you research into one library of markdown notes and indexes them so you can search them later. Before saving, it rates the sources by how trustworthy their domain is, checks the numbers and citations in the claims, and records a single confidence mark on the entry. Over time it flags notes that have gone stale and rebuilds hub pages by topic, tag, and project.

stratagem App

You give it a research question and it plans the work, then runs twelve specialist agents that read documents, scrape web pages and download company filings from the SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission). A synthesiser and a critic turn their findings into structured sections. Those sections render straight into finished files, including real PowerPoint decks and spreadsheets.

Nobody has this yet

Capabilities absent or incomplete across every subject.

  • Linking people, companies and products across documents

    The best score anywhere is 2 out of 3. The research plugin links notes to each other and rebuilds topic pages, but it never names the things inside them. The market research platform defines entity extraction and a graph store, but no live code path reaches them, so the graph stays empty and no relationship is ever written. Nobody connects the same company across two documents, and nobody draws the result.

  • Insight detection that does not depend on a prompt

    Contradiction, trend, comparison and gap detection exist only as written instructions the model is asked to follow. The closest things to code are local-smartz's bounded fact-check-and-redo loop and the research plugin's claim verifier. Nothing compares stored claims across entries.

  • Collection that runs on a schedule

    Every repo waits to be asked. market-intel was meant to be the feed reader and is dead. There is no watcher, timer or feed reader anywhere, and your existing background-job setup is the obvious place to put one.

  • Web search when no person is watching

    Only local-smartz and stratagem can search the web from code. The research plugin deliberately leaves search to the assistant hosting it, which works while you are in a session and disappears the moment a job runs unattended.

  • One contract for output length

    The writers exist; what is missing is a single rule that says long report, short report, key highlights or slide deck, and routes each to the right template. The research plugin's output rules and stratagem's format-routing table are the two halves of it.
